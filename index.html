About

A little piece of space,
on your desk

Desktop Satellite is a small hand-built model that behaves like the real thing — it captures light with a solar panel, converts it into power, and runs on its own with no cable attached. Every board, wire and solder joint was put together by hand.

On its onboard screen it shows the current temperature and humidity of the room, so the tiny satellite doubles as a living weather station that sits quietly beside your monitor.

It started as a way to practice soldering and electronics — turning raw components into something that actually works. The result is a small piece of “space” on the desk: a machine powered by the sun, sensing the room, and always quietly on.

Because it needs no cable and stores its own energy, it can be placed anywhere — by a window for maximum light, or anywhere a bit of light reaches. It’s the kind of project that makes an ordinary desk feel a little more like mission control.

Build journey

From parts to a tiny satellite

1

Start with the display & serial port

The first step was soldering the display and serial port onto the board — the “face” and the connection point the satellite would use to talk to the outside world.

2

Build the main frame

Next, the overall body was assembled and soldered together — the frame that holds everything in place and gives the satellite its shape.

3

Add the temperature & humidity sensor

Then the sensor was soldered in, giving the satellite its “sense of smell” — the ability to read the temperature and humidity of the room around it.

4

Add the battery — the final result

The battery was added so the satellite could store energy and keep running on its own — the moment it truly became a working desktop satellite.

5

Add the solar panel

The solar panel was fitted on — the satellite’s energy source. From here on, it could start catching light and turning it into power.

6

Wire & solder the bracket

The bracket was wired in and soldered into place, giving the satellite a solid point to anchor its moving parts and keep them steady.

7

Assemble the final frame

Everything was locked into its final frame — the outer shell that holds the whole build together and gives the satellite its finished look.

8

Wire up the internals

Finally, the inside was wired and routed so that power and data could flow cleanly between the panel, the sensor, the screen and the brain of the build.

Inspired by space

The same science, desk-sized

This isn’t just a model — it works on the same physics and engineering principles as the satellites flying in orbit. Everything real spacecraft do, this one does on a desk.

☀️

Solar power

Real craft: unfold solar panels to turn sunlight into electricity for every system.

This one: a small photovoltaic panel does the same — no cable needed.

🌡️

Environment sensing

Real craft: measure temperature, radiation and more to keep themselves healthy.

This one: reads the room’s temperature and humidity around the clock.

📡

Telemetry

Real craft: radio their data back to mission control on Earth.

This one: “relays” its readings straight to the onboard display.

🔋

Stored energy

Real craft: run on batteries charged by the sun to survive the dark side of orbit.

This one: stores its solar charge in a battery too, so it keeps working all evening.

Space facts

A few things you may not know

📦

CubeSats are real desk-satellites. Miniature “CubeSats” — often just 10×10×10 cm (about a shoebox) — are launched for research and education. The desk-size idea isn’t far from reality.

🏆

Solar cells have been to space since 1958. NASA’s Vanguard 1 was one of the first satellites powered partly by solar cells, and the technology has powered spacecraft ever since.

🌗

Orbit swings between extremes. A satellite in sunlight can hit about +120 °C, then plunge to about −120 °C in Earth’s shadow — which is why real spacecraft take thermal control so seriously.

⚡

The photovoltaic effect. A solar panel turns light into electricity using the photovoltaic effect — the same physics your desk panel is using right now.
